## The deliverable: the Artwork Passport

Every engagement with Ars Accordia results in **Artwork Passports** — permanent, standards-based identity documents for individual works of art.

A passport is not an inventory spreadsheet, a condition report, or a valuation. It is a single authoritative record that consolidates everything needed to identify, prove, protect, and transfer a work of art:

| What it records | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Full identity & attribution | Insurance, resale, loan eligibility |
| Provenance history | Legal title, due diligence, customs |
| Physical description & condition | Conservation decisions, transport protocols |
| Authority links (Wikidata, Getty ULAN, VIAF) | Scholarship, third-party verification |
| Permanent ArtBase ID | Unchanging reference across all contexts |
| Machine-readable export (LIDO 1.1 / EODEM) | Museum system interoperability |

Each passport is published at a stable URL on `arsaccordia.com` and listed in the public Ars Accordia catalogue — making it permanently discoverable by scholars, insurers, auction houses, and institutions.

## Who we work with

**Corporate collections**
Art held as company asset — typically undocumented beyond an inventory spreadsheet, rarely attributed beyond the obvious, and absent from any published catalogue. We provide the documentation infrastructure for board reporting, insurance, loan eligibility, and eventual deaccession or donation.

**Private collectors**
Single-owner collections of 10–200 works. Often acquired over decades, with incomplete provenance and no single authoritative record. We produce a catalogue that consolidates the collection's identity and survives the collector.

**Estates and foundations**
Collections at the point of transition — inheritance, sale, donation, or bequest. The Artwork Passport is the legal and scholarly document the estate needs before transfer. We work from existing documentation where it exists, and research gaps where it does not.

**What we do not do:** marketplace listings, valuations, insurance appraisals, or authentication of contested attributions. We catalogue and document; we do not certify value.

## The process

### Phase 1 — Consultation (free)

A 45–60 minute conversation to understand the collection: what you hold, what documentation already exists, what the collection needs (insurance, estate planning, loans, scholarship, public visibility), and what a realistic scope looks like.

Outcome: a clear scope, a cost estimate, and a project timeline.

### Phase 2 — Cataloguing

For each work: physical examination or review of existing documentation; research into attribution, provenance, and authority records; data entry to Ars Accordia cataloguing standards (ICOM Object ID, CDWA).

For each artist represented: authority research against Getty ULAN, Wikidata, VIAF, and RKD; creation or linkage of an authority record.

Timeline: 2–4 weeks for a collection of 10–25 works, depending on documentation depth.

### Phase 3 — Passport generation

Each catalogued work receives a generated Artwork Passport: a permanent HTML document, a machine-readable LIDO 1.1 XML record, and a stable URL. Each passport carries the Ars Accordia validation badge showing which Object ID fields are complete.

We return a package to the client including: all passports as PDF-ready HTML, the LIDO XML exports, and a summary report.

### Phase 4 — Publication and registry

With client approval, passports are published on `arsaccordia.com` and listed in the public catalogue. Authority links to Wikidata are created or updated. The collection appears in the public Ars Accordia registry — discoverable by scholarship, institutions, and aggregators.

Clients may choose private mode (passports generated but not published) for collections not yet ready for public visibility.

## Standards compliance

Ars Accordia passports comply with or reference:

- **ICOM Object ID** — the international minimum standard for artwork identification
- **CDWA** — Categories for the Description of Works of Art (Getty Research Institute)
- **LIDO 1.1** — Lightweight Information Describing Objects (CIDOC)
- **EODEM** — LIDO profile for European museum loans
- **Getty AAT** — Art & Architecture Thesaurus (object types, materials, techniques)
- **Getty ULAN** — Union List of Artist Names
- **Wikidata** — open knowledge graph; confirmed authority links published as Wikidata statements
