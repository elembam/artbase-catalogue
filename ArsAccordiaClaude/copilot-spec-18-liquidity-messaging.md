# Instruction 18 — Make liquidity & transferability the site's key message

*Hand to Copilot as a site-wide copy-and-framing task (not a build). The site carries the object-anchor concept — the **contribution** — but leads with the wrong emotion for the owner. Split the message by audience: **owners want custody** (safe, known, provable, theirs); **advisors want liquidity** (the work changes hands with less friction). Same passport, two doors. Lead owner-facing copy with **security and command**; keep liquidity/transferability for advisor-facing copy. The standing discipline holds: **liquidity, not fungibility; we document and anchor, we never value or authenticate.** Where this conflicts with Copilot's instinct, the spec wins — ask before deviating.*

---

## The key formulation — split by audience

**Owner-facing lead (the top line):**

> **Secure the record.**

*Secure* carries both meanings on purpose: make the collection safe, and lock down each work's identity. It is custody and anchoring in two words, with no hint of "sell." Everything owner-facing hangs off it — *know exactly what you hold, prove it's yours, protect it, and be ready for whatever comes.* "Ready for whatever comes" quietly includes insurance, succession, a loss, a loan, a sale — without naming the sale as the goal.

**Advisor-facing line (lawyers, family offices, banks):**

> **A work nobody can properly identify is hard to insure, inherit, sell, or lend. A documented one is simple to deal with — we don't set what it's worth, we make it ready when it's needed.**

Plain words only. **Do not** use "liquid," "fungible," "liquidity," "transferable asset," or "anchored enough to move" — that vocabulary sounds like jargon and reads as inauthentic. Say plainly what documentation does: it makes a work easy to insure, inherit, sell, divide, donate, or lend when the time comes.

**The rule:** owners hear *security and command*; advisors hear *easy to deal with when needed*. For owners, this readiness is only ever a consequence they control, never the pitch.

---

## The conceptual chain (the logic the whole site should express)

```
Secure the record   →   the work is anchored & provable   →   the owner is in command and prepared   →   ready for whatever comes
(the contribution)      (cited, verifiable identity)        (custody, control)                         (insurance · succession · loss · loan · division · sale)
```

For the **advisor**, the same chain reads from the transaction end: a provable, documented record → the work is *simple to deal with* when a decision comes. Same documentation, described from the side the reader stands on. Every page sits somewhere on this chain and makes the next link visible.

---

## Where to apply it

- **Hero / top-line.** Lead with **"Secure the record."** — custody and command, not motion. Never pair *anchor* with a movement word; the headline should land *safety and control*, with readiness held in reserve, not the exit.
- **"Why we exist" page.** Use the updated statement (now ends on liquidity as what the anchor earns).
- **Method / scoring page.** Connect the score to transfer-readiness explicitly: a better-documented work is a more transferable one; the score is a proxy for how ready the work is to change hands. (Do **not** restate the score as a value or price — see guardrails.)
- **Collections / dossier / service pages.** Frame the deliverable as **readiness**: the dossier is the record an owner can hand to an insurer, lawyer, valuer, or heir; the assessment tells them how complete that record is and what's missing.
- **Advisor-facing copy and the outreach emails.** Lead with the moment the advisor is managing (succession, insurance, a pending sale) — that moment *is* the liquidity test.

---

## Reframe the product logic, not only the words

Make the framing consistent under the copy:

- **The passport = the anchor** = the thing that makes a work referenceable, and therefore transferable.
- **The score = how complete the record is** — how well-documented and cross-referenced the work/collection is. A measure of readiness, never of worth.
- **The gap map = what the record is still missing** — the specific documentation a work needs before it can be cleanly insured, inherited, sold, or lent.
- Wherever the audience is an owner or advisor, recast benefit language from the **feature** ("standards-based documentation," "authority links") to the plain **outcome** ("easy to insure, inherit, sell, divide, donate, or lend when the time comes"). Keep the feature language for the method/technical pages where it earns trust.

---

## Discipline guardrails (do not cross these)

- **Plain words, no asset jargon.** Do not use "liquid," "liquidity," "fungible," "transferable asset," or "anchored enough to move" anywhere on the site — it sounds inauthentic. Say what documentation plainly does: makes a work easy to insure, inherit, sell, divide, donate, or lend. Never imply works are interchangeable; each stays singular.
- **We document and anchor; we do not value or authenticate.** "Easier to **value**" means easier for a valuer — Ars Accordia never values or appraises, and never authenticates. Wherever "value" appears in the liquidity list, the neutrality line ("we do not judge, authenticate, or value the work ourselves") must be intact on the same page.
- **Reduce friction; don't claim to create worth.** Ars Accordia makes a work easier to *transfer*; it does not make it more *valuable*. No copy may imply the passport raises a work's price or worth.
- **No overclaim of outcome.** A documented work is *more transferable*, not *guaranteed to sell, insure, or settle*. Keep the claim to friction reduction.
- **Never pair *anchor* with *move*.** "Anchor" keeps its technical sense (anchoring an object's identity) and never appears beside a movement word — an anchor stops motion; the pun contradicts the message.
- **Owners hear safety, not sale.** Owner-facing copy never leads with selling, moving, or liquidity. The owner's felt need is custody; transfer-readiness is preparedness held in reserve, surfaced only as an option they control.

---

## Acceptance / done

1. The owner-facing hero leads with **"Secure the record."** (or a faithful custody/command variant); liquidity language appears only on advisor-facing copy, never as the owner's lead.
2. The method/scoring, collections, dossier, and service pages each express the secure → documented → ready chain, framing documentation as a complete, usable record.
3. No page uses asset jargon ("liquid," "fungible," etc.), and none implies Ars Accordia values, appraises, authenticates, or raises the worth of a work.
4. The "we do not value/authenticate" neutrality line is present wherever the liquidity language (especially the word "value") appears.
5. Feature language (standards, authority links, scope) remains on the technical/method pages where it builds trust; plain outcome language (easy to insure, inherit, sell, lend when needed) leads on the owner/advisor-facing pages.
