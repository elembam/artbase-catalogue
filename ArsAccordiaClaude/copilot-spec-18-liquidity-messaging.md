# Instruction 18 — Make liquidity & transferability the site's key message

*Hand to Copilot as a site-wide copy-and-framing task (not a build). The site already carries the object-anchor concept — the **contribution**. What it under-states is the **payoff**: a well-anchored work is a more liquid, more transferable one. Adopt that as the site's key commercial message and thread it consistently through copy and through how the product's logic is framed. The standing discipline holds throughout: **liquidity, not fungibility; we document and anchor, we never value or authenticate.** Where this conflicts with Copilot's instinct, the spec wins — ask before deviating.*

---

## The key formulation (the message everything points at)

> **A well-anchored, well-documented work changes hands with less friction — easier to insure, value, move, divide, sell, donate, and lend.**

and, for the asset-literate audience (advisors, family offices, banks):

> **We don't make art fungible — nothing does, and its singularity is the point. We make it liquid: anchored and documented enough to move.**

This second line is a differentiator, not a throwaway: it shows the reader Ars Accordia understands the asset class precisely. Use it where the audience is financial/advisory.

---

## The conceptual chain (the logic the whole site should express)

```
Anchor the object   →   it becomes referenceable   →   it becomes liquid / transferable   →   which is what is tested at the moments that matter
(the contribution)      (cited, verifiable)            (changes hands with less friction)     (inheritance · insurance · sale · division · donation · loan)
```

Every page should sit somewhere on this chain and make the next link visible. The contribution (anchoring/documentation) is the *means*; liquidity/transferability is the *end the buyer feels*.

---

## Where to apply it

- **Hero / top-line.** Pair the contribution with the payoff in one breath: *we anchor the individual work — so it can move.* The headline should land the payoff, not just the method.
- **"Why we exist" page.** Use the updated statement (now ends on liquidity as what the anchor earns).
- **Method / scoring page.** Connect the score to transfer-readiness explicitly: a better-documented work is a more transferable one; the score is a proxy for how ready the work is to change hands. (Do **not** restate the score as a value or price — see guardrails.)
- **Collections / dossier / service pages.** Frame the deliverable as **transfer-readiness**: the dossier is what makes a collection deal-ready; the assessment tells an owner how close their collection is to moving without friction.
- **Advisor-facing copy and the outreach emails.** Lead with the moment the advisor is managing (succession, insurance, a pending sale) — that moment *is* the liquidity test.

---

## Reframe the product logic, not only the words

Make the framing consistent under the copy:

- **The passport = the anchor** = the thing that makes a work referenceable, and therefore transferable.
- **The score = a transfer-readiness proxy** — how well-anchored and documented the work/collection is. (A measure of readiness to move, never a measure of worth.)
- **The gap map = what stands between the work and friction-free transfer** — the specific documentation a work still needs before it can change hands cleanly.
- Wherever the audience is an owner or advisor, recast benefit language from the **feature** ("standards-based documentation," "authority links") to the **outcome** ("easier to insure, divide, sell, donate, lend"). Keep the feature language for the method/technical pages where it earns trust.

---

## Discipline guardrails (do not cross these)

- **Liquidity, not fungibility.** Never imply works are interchangeable or substitutable. Each work stays singular; that singularity is preserved and is the point. The word "fungible" appears on the site only in the construction *"we don't make art fungible — we make it liquid."*
- **We document and anchor; we do not value or authenticate.** "Easier to **value**" means easier for a valuer — Ars Accordia never values or appraises, and never authenticates. Wherever "value" appears in the liquidity list, the neutrality line ("we do not judge, authenticate, or value the work ourselves") must be intact on the same page.
- **Reduce friction; don't claim to create worth.** Ars Accordia makes a work easier to *transfer*; it does not make it more *valuable*. No copy may imply the passport raises a work's price or worth.
- **No overclaim of outcome.** A documented work is *more transferable*, not *guaranteed to sell, insure, or settle*. Keep the claim to friction reduction.

---

## Acceptance / done

1. The key formulation (or a faithful variant) appears on the hero and the "why we exist" page; the payoff — not only the method — is what the top-line lands.
2. The method/scoring, collections, dossier, and service pages each express the anchor → liquid → moments chain, framing documentation as transfer-readiness.
3. No page implies fungibility, or that Ars Accordia values, appraises, authenticates, or raises the worth of a work.
4. The "we do not value/authenticate" neutrality line is present wherever the liquidity language (especially the word "value") appears.
5. Feature language (standards, authority links, scope) remains on the technical/method pages where it builds trust; outcome language (liquidity, transferability, friction) leads on the owner/advisor-facing pages.
