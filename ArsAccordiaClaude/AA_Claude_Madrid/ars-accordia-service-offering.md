## What Ars Accordia does

Ars Accordia **measures and improves the documentation health of art collections.** We give a collection a single, defensible **Documentation Score** — and a program to raise it over time.

Underneath, the work is standards-based cataloguing: each work becomes a permanent **Artwork Passport** linking identity, provenance, condition, and the authority records institutions rely on (Wikidata, Getty ULAN/AAT, VIAF, national libraries). But what the collector holds, tracks, and improves is the **score** — a measurable KPI for something that has never been measurable before, and a number a board, an insurer, or an estate can actually manage.

We are a documentation and reference service — not a marketplace, a gallery, or a valuation house.

---

## The Collection Documentation Score

The score is the heart of the offering. It answers a question every serious owner has and none can currently answer: *how well documented is my collection, really?*

- It is measured against the collection's **full defined extent** — every work in scope, not just the ones we've catalogued — so it reflects reality and can't be inflated by leaving works out.
- It combines three things: **Coverage** (how much of the collection is catalogued), **Completeness** (how full each record is — identity, provenance, condition, authority links, image), and **Corroboration** (how well each record is verified against independent authorities, on our four-level standard).
- It produces a **band** — from *Inventory Only* to *Fully Documented* — with a percentage and a **gap map** showing exactly where the score is lost.
- It is **private by default** (a confidential KPI for the owner) and **public only with consent** (a credential the collection can display).

The method is the same for every collection and is published in full. A score you can trust is one whose working you can see.

## How it works — the documentation loop

The score turns documentation from an open-ended expense into a managed program with a target, a plan, and visible progress. The engagement is a loop:

1. **Assess** — establish the collection's scope, score it as it stands today, and deliver the gap map and a proposal to reach the next band.
2. **Improve** — catalogue the works and fill the gaps that raise the score; re-score at milestones, so the number climbs visibly.
3. **Maintain** — as the collection grows and authorities evolve, new acquisitions are catalogued and records re-validated, so the score is held rather than eroded.

Each band reached sets the next target; each gap map writes the next plan. That is what makes the relationship ongoing rather than a single transaction — the score ratchets, and maintaining it is continuous.

## Who it's for

- **Corporate collections** — holdings that have grown over years without systematic documentation, now needing a defensible record for governance, insurance, and reporting.
- **Private collections** — inherited or assembled works needing authoritative records.
- **Estates** — documentation for succession, division, insurance, and provenance.
- **Institutions** — foundations, law firms, banks, and universities holding art outside a museum infrastructure.

The recurring situation: significant works, no measurable record. When the person who knew the collection leaves, the knowledge leaves with them. The score makes that knowledge an asset that is owned, tracked, and improved.

## Step 1 — The Collection Assessment

The entry point, and a complete deliverable in itself. We:

- **Establish scope** — agree the collection's full extent and produce a definitive inventory (the denominator the score is measured against).
- **Score the collection as it stands** — a baseline Documentation Score and band.
- **Deliver the gap map** — where the score is lost: provenance documented for X% of works, condition for Y%, authority links for Z%, and so on.
- **Propose the path to the next band** — the specific work it would take to move from, say, *Partially Documented* to *Substantially Documented*, with scope and cost.

The Assessment is discrete, fixed-scope, and low-commitment. It gives a collector a number and a roadmap on its own — and it is what makes the program that follows concrete rather than open-ended.

---

## Step 2 — The Documentation Program

The core engagement: the cataloguing that raises the score toward an agreed **target band.** Each work researched and catalogued to standard, authorities reconciled, provenance and condition documented and sourced, passports issued with permanent IDs. We **re-score at milestones**, so progress is visible the whole way.

How deep each work is catalogued is itself a lever on how fast and how high the score climbs:

- **Essential depth** — identity, authority links, structured data: the core record.
- **Documented depth** — adds sourced provenance and a condition report: the full passport.

A program targets a band; the depth and the number of works are set to reach it within scope and budget.

---

## Step 3 — Maintenance & Monitoring

Once a target band is reached, an optional ongoing engagement keeps it there: new acquisitions catalogued (so coverage stays complete as the collection grows), records re-validated as the authorities they rest on change, and the score monitored and reported on a regular cadence. For an institution or corporate collection, this is the line item that keeps documentation from quietly decaying.

## What sits underneath: the Artwork Passport

Each catalogued work receives a permanent **Artwork Passport** (`AP-YYYY-NNNNNN`, never reused) — the unit of work that moves the score. A complete passport records identity (to ICOM Object ID and Getty CDWA), authority links graded for corroboration, sourced provenance, condition, and structured/export data (Schema.org for discovery; LIDO/EODEM for museum and loan exchange). Human-readable, machine-readable, permanently identified.

## Visibility & privacy

You control each work's and the collection's visibility: **public** (in the registry / a published credential), **restricted** (by link), or **private** (your workspace only). A public score is published **only with your consent.** Personal data of living individuals — owners above all — is held privately by default and never published without explicit consent.

## What this is — and isn't

- **It is:** a measured, improvable documentation standard for collections; standards-based cataloguing; sourced provenance and condition; permanent identifiers; graded authority links.
- **It isn't:** a marketplace or sales channel; an appraisal or valuation service (available only through an independent partner); legal advice. And we **never** publish a score for a collection we were not engaged to assess, or without consent.

The neutrality is deliberate: our value is trust in the record and the score, and that trust depends on our being a documentation service rather than a party with an interest in the work.
